\chapter{Modern Quantum Computing in 2026}

This chapter summarizes topics a serious introductory student should know beyond foundational algorithms. Statements are tagged by evidence type; company roadmaps are not scientific facts.

\section{Physical Qubit Platforms}

Leading platforms include superconducting circuits, trapped ions, neutral atoms, photonics, and spin qubits. Each trades coherence time, gate fidelity, connectivity, and scalability differently. No platform has yet demonstrated fault-tolerant logical computation at scale suitable for cryptographically relevant factoring.

\section{Logical Qubits and the Surface Code}

The surface code \cite{acharya2024} is a leading topological code with local checks on a 2D lattice. Logical error rates can fall below physical rates when sufficiently large codes are used; resource estimates for useful chemistry or factoring remain substantial.

\section{Noise, Channels, and Mitigation}

Completely positive trace-preserving maps model open systems. NISQ devices benefit from calibration, dynamical decoupling, and error mitigation; fault tolerance requires redundancy and continual syndrome extraction.

\section{Quantum Advantage and Benchmarks}

Random circuit sampling milestones \cite{arute2019} demonstrate quantum behavior hard to simulate classically for specific tasks; this is not universal speedup for everyday computing. Always ask: \emph{which problem, which metric, which comparison?}

\section{Quantum Networking and Cryptography}

Quantum key distribution implements information-theoretic security assumptions under device trust models. Post-quantum \emph{classical} cryptography addresses Shor's threat to RSA/ECC with lattice-based and other schemes standardized separately from quantum hardware progress.

\section{Resource Estimation and Classical Simulation}

Classical simulation of $n$ qubits requires $O(2^n)$ complex amplitudes in general; Clifford circuits simulate efficiently. Understanding this gap clarifies why variational algorithms and mitigation target near-term hardware.

\section{Open Questions at the \cutoff{} Cutoff}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]

\item Which application domains yield durable quantum advantage.
