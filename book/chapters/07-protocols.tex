\chapter{Entanglement and Quantum Protocols}

Entanglement enables communication protocols impossible with classical bits alone, while respecting the no-signaling principle.

\section{EPR and Hidden Variables}

Entangled correlations prompted questions about local hidden variables. Quantum mechanics predicts correlations beyond any local classical model for certain measurements.

\section{CHSH Inequality}

Define correlations $E(a,b)$ for measurement settings $a,b$. The CHSH combination
\[
S=E(a,b)+E(a,b')+E(a',b)-E(a',b')\le 2
\]
for local hidden-variable theories. Quantum mechanics permits $|S|\le 2\sqrt2$ (Tsirelson bound).

\section{Superdense Coding}

Shared $\ket{\Phi^+}$ lets Alice encode two classical bits into one qubit by applying $I,X,Z,$ or $XZ$ before sending her qubit to Bob, who performs Bell decoding.

\section{Quantum Teleportation}

Unknown $\ket{\psi}=\alpha\ket{0}+\beta\ket{1}$ transfers to Bob using shared entanglement, local gates, measurement, and two classical bits. No matter moves; Alice's original state is destroyed; signaling faster than light is impossible.

\section{BB84 Quantum Key Distribution}

Alice encodes random bits in random Z or X bases; Bob measures in random bases. Matching bases yield sifted key bits; sampling reveals eavesdropping via elevated error rates.
