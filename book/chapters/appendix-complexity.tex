\section{Complexity Classes}

\textbf{P:} Decision problems solvable in polynomial time on a deterministic classical Turing machine.

\textbf{NP:} Decision problems whose yes-instances admit polynomial-size certificates verifiable in polynomial time.

\textbf{BQP:} Decision problems solvable in polynomial time on a quantum computer with error probability $<1/3$, equivalently bounded-error quantum polynomial time.

Known containments: $\mathrm{P}\subseteq\mathrm{BQP}\subseteq\mathrm{PSPACE}$. The relationship between \textbf{NP} and \textbf{BQP} is open; quantum computers are not known to solve NP-complete problems in polynomial time.

\section{Simulation Complexity}

\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
\item \textbf{General $n$-qubit state:} $O(2^n)$ amplitudes; exact simulation intractable for large $n$.
\item \textbf{Clifford circuits:} Gottesman--Knill simulation in $O(n^2)$ per layer (polynomial).
\item \textbf{Tensor networks:} structured states may simulate beyond $50$ qubits with approximations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Cautionary Notes}

Different algorithms provide different advantages. Quantum computers are not universally exponentially faster. Always specify: problem family, promise conditions, metric (queries, time, samples), and classical baseline.
