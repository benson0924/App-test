\chapter{Quantum Algorithms}

Quantum algorithms exploit interference and phase to outperform classical strategies for specific problems.

\section{Oracles and Phase Kickback}

Standard oracle: $U_f\ket{x}\ket{y}=\ket{x}\ket{y\oplus f(x)}$. With $\ket{y}=\ket{-}$, phase kickback yields $(-1)^{f(x)}\ket{x}\ket{-}$, implementing a phase oracle $O_f\ket{x}=(-1)^{f(x)}\ket{x}$.

\section{Deutsch and Deutsch--Jozsa}

For $f:\{0,1\}\to\{0,1\}$, Deutsch's algorithm distinguishes constant from balanced functions with one query versus two classically in the worst case. Deutsch--Jozsa generalizes to $n$ bits with one query versus up to $2^{n-1}+1$ classically.

\section{Bernstein--Vazirani}

Hidden string $s\in\{0,1\}^n$ with $f(x)=s\cdot x\bmod 2$ is found with one query quantumly versus $n$ classically.

\section{Simon's Algorithm}

Periodic functions $f(x)=f(x\oplus s)$ yield equations $y\cdot s=0$ over $\mathbb F_2$; enough samples determine $s$ via linear algebra.

\section{Grover Search}

Unstructured search over $N=2^n$ items uses $O(\sqrt N)$ oracle queries via alternating oracle $O_w=I-2\ket{w}\bra{w}$ and diffusion $D=2\ket{s}\bra{s}-I$. Optimal iterations $\approx \lfloor\frac{\pi}{4}\sqrt N\rfloor$.

\section{Quantum Fourier Transform}

\[
\mathrm{QFT}_N\ket{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}e^{2\pi ixk/N}\ket{k}.
\]
The QFT is unitary and efficiently implementable, but amplitudes are not all readable at once.

\section{Quantum Phase Estimation}

Given $U\ket{u}=e^{2\pi i\phi}\ket{u}$, controlled powers of $U$ and inverse QFT estimate $\phi$ to $m$ bits using $m$ ancilla qubits.

\section{Shor's Factoring Algorithm}

Factoring reduces to finding period $r$ of $f(x)=a^x\bmod N$. For $N=15$, $a=2$, we find $r=4$, then $\gcd(2^{r/2}\pm 1,N)\in\{3,5\}$. Quantum speedup lies in period finding; remaining steps are classical \cite{shor1994}.
